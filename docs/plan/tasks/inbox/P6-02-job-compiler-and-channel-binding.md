# P6-02 Job compiler and channel binding

Phase: 6 | Milestone: M9 | Depends on: `P6-01`, `P3-06` | Size: M (S: a day or two, M: up to a week, L: more)

## Goal

Compiling a job for a multi-path machine: the wait codes in the machine's form and the words that only one path may write.

## Scope

- Per channel a compile with the shared `TargetState` awareness; `SYNC` into `M1xx` with `P` path lists or bitmasks (`[sync] paths`), `start_mark`, groups; `WAITM(mark, channels)` on Siemens.
- Channel-bound functions (`channel = n`, `channels = "all"`): move the word to the owning channel's program or duplicate it into every channel behind a generated `SYNC` (D56); a single-channel compile of such a word is an ERROR.
- Output: one file per channel with the machine's naming (`O1000`, `O1000.P-2`; `_C1`/`_C2`).

- The job runs STATIC once before it is written (`JobCompiler.Run.cs`), with the `JobRunner` of P6-01 as `ncx check --job` runs it, every executed block raising `BLOCK_WRITE`. A listener per channel (`ChannelMarks`) records the marks it passes (`SYNC_RELEASE`: mark, round, channels) and, for every block of its program, how many marks it had passed. Two channels pass the same mark when their releases have the same mark, round and channels; that is how "the same mark" is found in the program of another channel. A job the run stops (the deadlock `VM571`, a channel the manifest names twice, an ERROR in a program) reports what `check --job` reports and writes nothing; otherwise the run reports only the rules of the job scheduler (`VM571`, `VM572`, `VM850` to `VM854`), and every other finding comes from the compile of the channel program that holds the block, so nothing is reported twice.
- Per channel a compile through the compiler of the family (`JobChannel.Build`): the program the manifest names, the subprograms its walk called, the blocks the job compiler inserted and without the words it took out, every section on the channel of the job. Two channels may run two programs of one file; each file holds its own program. A subprogram that no channel program calls stands in no channel file, with the WARNING `CMP720`. The compile gets a view of the job (`CompileOptions.Job`, a `JobView` with the channel and the channels of the job) and the machine as the job sees it: `[machine] channels` are the channels of the job, so that a `SYNC` without `WITH` waits for them (language 4.8, `P12` on a three-path machine running two), and `[sync] mark_range` is left to the job compiler, which checks every mark against it and the groups. The compilers of the families write the wait code as before, from `[sync]` and `[machine] channels`; no family file changed. Each channel is written with the target state of its own program, one program per path, as the job scheduler keeps the state per channel (the `TODO(question)` of `JobRunner.Add`, D219).
- `SYNC` in the program of a channel of a job with several channels is no `SYNC` of a single-channel job: `VmOptions.JobChannels`, which `CompilerBase` sets from the view, so `VM570` is not raised in a job compile.
- `start_mark` (machine-config 5): a `SYNC` of the mark after the `PROGRAM=BEGIN` of every channel program, unless every channel already passes it first with every channel of the job, as the Nakamura WY pair does with `M199` after its setup blocks. `[sync] groups`: a mark lies within the range of the first group that holds all its channels, else within `mark_range` (`CMP710`).
- Channel binding (VM 3.8 rule 2a, D56; `ChannelBinding.cs`): a word is bound when the table it is written from has `channel = n` or `channels = "all"`: `[spindle.ROLE]` for `SPINDLE`, `RPM`, `ORIENT`, `CSS`, `VC`, `RPM_MAX` (the role of the default spindle without an address), `[spindle_mode.ROLE]`, `[spindle_sync]` for `SPINDLE_SYNC` and its `PHASE`, `[coolant]` (`STANDARD` without an address), `[func]`. A word bound to channel n in the program of another channel leaves its block (the block stays with its other words, and goes when only `SKIP` would remain) and stands, as a block of its own, directly after the `SYNC` of the same mark in the program of channel n, or after its `PROGRAM=BEGIN` when no mark stands before the word. A word bound to every channel gets a generated `SYNC` before its block and, in every other program, the `SYNC` and the word after the same mark; the mark is the first of the range of all channels that the job does not use. The moved and generated blocks are generated blocks (language 4.15): a diagnostic on one names the block it stands after as its line and the block the word came from as `OriginLine`, `file(5, from 6)`, the risk of the phase (D98); a skipped block's words and `SYNC` stay skipped. The ERRORs: a channel the job does not run (`CMP702`), a bound word or its mark in a subprogram (`CMP703`), a channel that takes no part in the mark before the word (`CMP704`), no wait or range for the generated `SYNC` (`CMP711`). A single-channel compile (`CompilerBase.Compile`, any family) reports a word bound to another channel than its program's (`CMP700`) and a word bound to every channel on a machine of several channels (`CMP701`); a compile of a channel program of a job reports a word bound to another channel as well, which catches a word an expansion rule generates.

- Words an expansion rule or a rewriter generates are bound (virtual machine 3.8 rule 2a: "every function the compiler writes"; D56). Before, the job compiler bound only the words of the parsed file and the family compiler expanded the channel program afterwards, so a `channels = "all"` word of `[tool_change] pre` stood in one program without a wait (exit 0), and a generated word bound to another channel was `CMP700` telling the user to compile the job. What the parser and the expander report on a file goes once to the compile of the first channel program of the file; an ERROR of the expander stops the job before the run, as one of the parser does.
